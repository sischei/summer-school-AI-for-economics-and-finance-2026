The certificate: a margin, not a number

A credit committee must hold, review, or reduce a facility. The model supplies one estimate. The question is not what it recommends — it is whether that recommendation may be executed without a human.

Epistemic status: exact arithmetic on a declared 3×2 loss matrix. The certificate is the sufficient condition R(a,p̂) − R(a₁,p̂) > ε (rg(a₁)+rg(a)) proved in the session; the flip counter below re-checks it by brute force on 4000 draws inside the ball each redraw. Nothing here validates p̂ or ε — supplying those honestly is the hard part, and it is your job, not the widget's.

Predict first. review costs 0.6 whichever way the world goes — its loss range is zero, the safest row in the matrix. reduce carries a range of 1.0 and hold a range of 6.0. Now raise ε until certificates start disappearing. Rank the three by which survives longest, and commit before you drag. (Each one does close eventually — the question is the order, and at what ε.)

Debrief — part one: the recommendation is not the thing that changes. Hold p̂ fixed at 0.25 and raise ε. The preferred action stays review at every setting — the estimate never moves, so neither does the argmin. What disappears is the right to execute it without a human. Those are two different objects, and a system that reports only the first has not told you whether it may act.

Debrief — part two: own exposure does not order survival. Geometry does. At the default loss matrix the certified regions are, exactly: hold on [0, 0.1−ε), closing at ε = 0.10; review on (0.1+ε, 0.4−ε), closing at ε = 0.15; reduce on (0.4+ε, 1], closing at ε = 0.60 — where even a certain default, p̂ = 1, stops clearing the margin against review.

So the zero-exposure action does not survive longest, and the highest-exposure action does not die first for the reason you would guess. What decides survival is how much room an action's optimality region has. review is squeezed between two crossings 0.3 apart and loses ε from each end, so it closes first at 0.15; reduce has 0.6 of room to the right-hand edge of the simplex, so it closes last. A hedging action — the one a committee reaches for precisely when it is unsure — is the one that can only be automated when you are already sure.

Note also what the middle column of the table shows: review's required margin is set entirely by the loss range of the alternative it is declining. Your right to act is destroyed by the exposure you are choosing not to take.
