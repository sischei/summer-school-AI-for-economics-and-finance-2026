DPO: an exact cancellation

The partition function really does vanish — you can watch it. What survives is every modelling assumption it was standing next to.

Epistemic status: exact arithmetic on a six-response toy prompt. The reference policy, the latent rewards and the lengths are stipulated; everything derived from them — the Gibbs tilt, the implicit reward, the Bradley–Terry probabilities, the KL — is computed exactly, not fitted. No model is trained here.

Debrief — part one. r̂ − r is the same number for every response: it is −β log Z(x), a function of the prompt alone. Both responses in a comparison share the prompt, so it cancels in the difference and the two preference columns agree to machine precision. The intractable normaliser never has to be computed.

Debrief — part two: what did not cancel. Now raise δ. The implicit reward is a sum of per-token log-ratios, so a policy that adds even a tiny constant to every token's log-ratio gains β·δ·ℓ — proportional to length. Nothing in the algebra forbids it, and the objective rewards it: the winner flips to a longer response and E[tokens] climbs, with the true rewards untouched. Tick length-normalisation and the ranking comes back. The cancellation removed a computation; the modelling commitments — Bradley–Terry, a single latent reward, a fixed correct πref, and offline pairs that cover the deployed distribution — are all still load-bearing.
