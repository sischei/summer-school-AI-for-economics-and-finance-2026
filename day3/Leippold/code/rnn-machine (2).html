Recurrence: a product, not a sum

Backpropagation through time multiplies one Jacobian by itself, over and over. Powers do exactly two things — and the upper bound you can prove is not the behaviour you will measure.

Epistemic status: an exact numerical simulation of a seeded 8-dimensional tanh RNN. Every curve is the measured norm of a real Jacobian product, not a fitted illustration. Nothing here is trained — the point is that the failure is present at initialization.

Predict first. The largest singular value of W starts at 1.05 — above one, so the bound (γσ)k permits growth. At lag 50, does the measured gradient vanish, explode, or stay roughly put? Commit before you touch a slider.

Debrief. At σ = 1.05 the bound is 1.035 — it permits growth — while the measured product falls twelve orders of magnitude over fifty steps. Two things separate them. The per-step gains γj = ‖diag(tanh′(aj))‖ are strictly below one away from the origin — note sup|tanh′| = 1, so tanh buys nothing in the uniform bound and everything along an actual trajectory — and submultiplicativity is loose: a product of random matrices decays at its Lyapunov exponent, well below σmax. Note too that σmax is the operator norm, an upper bound on the spectral radius; a bound above one is permission, not a prediction. Explosion needs roughly σ ≥ 2.3 here, so the vanishing regime is not a knife-edge — it is nearly everywhere. Clipping flattens the explosion and leaves the vanishing untouched, which is why the fix cannot be an optimizer. The cyan line is the fix: an additive carry path whose Jacobian is a diagonal near one.
