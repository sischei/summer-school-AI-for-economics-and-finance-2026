LoRA: a subspace, not a leash

The low-rank update is the standard way to adapt a model cheaply. It is also routinely mistaken for a safety mechanism, and the arithmetic says otherwise.

Epistemic status: exact linear algebra on a 32×32 target update built from a declared spectrum, so the rank-r truncation is the Eckart–Young optimum by construction rather than a numerical approximation. Parameter counts are reported both for this toy and at a realistic d = k = 4096. Nothing here is trained.

Debrief — part one: the answer is no. Hold the rank at 1 and drag the magnitude. rank(BA) never moves; ‖BA‖F grows without bound. Formally, take B = c·e1 and A = c·e1T: the product has rank one and Frobenius norm c². Rank restricts which directions the weights may move in. It places no bound whatever on how far. Catastrophic forgetting at r = 4 is entirely possible, and if you want a magnitude constraint you must add one — weight decay on the adapter, or an explicit KL to the base model's outputs.

Debrief — part two: read the sweep, don't just tune it. Now move γ. A fast-decaying spectrum means a handful of directions carry the update: r = 4 captures nearly everything and the residual goes dark. Flatten the spectrum and the same r captures almost nothing — the residual is as bright as the target. So the rank sweep is not hyperparameter tuning; it is a measurement of how far your domain sits from the base model. Loss still falling at r = 64 says the required update is genuinely high-dimensional, and domain-adaptive pretraining is probably the cheaper instrument. Flat beyond r = 4 says you were only adjusting a task boundary.
